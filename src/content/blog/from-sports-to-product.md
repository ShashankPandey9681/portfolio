Before I became a product manager, I was an athlete. And honestly, the transition wasn't as big a leap as people think.

## What carries over

Sports teach you things no MBA ever will:

- **Read the game** — On the field, you learn to see patterns before they fully form. In product, that's pattern-matching across user behavior, market signals, and data.
- **Train, don't just perform** — Matches are won in practice. Products are won in preparation — the research, the prototyping, the unglamorous repetitions.
- **Lose fast, learn faster** — Every athlete knows the sting of a bad game. You debrief, adjust, and show up tomorrow. Same energy for failed experiments.

## The biggest difference

In sports, the scoreboard is immediate. In product, you might wait months to know if you were right. That patience — sitting with ambiguity — was the hardest skill to develop.

But the fire? That stays the same.
